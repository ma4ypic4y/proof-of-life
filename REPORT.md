# Proof of Life — report

This report covers the methods and numbers behind the [README](README.md). Every figure below comes from the files in [`data/`](data), [`web/data/`](web/data) and [`lean/`](lean). The tables were generated by [`analysis/report_tables.py`](analysis/report_tables.py).

## 1. Setup

**The soup.** This is the BFF soup of Agüera y Arcas et al. (2024). A world holds 131,072 programs of 64 random bytes. Each epoch the programs are paired at random. Each pair is glued into one 128-byte tape, run for at most 8,192 steps, and split back. Ten byte values are instructions:

- `[ ]` loop while the byte under head 0 is non-zero
- `.` copies head 0 to head 1
- `,` copies head 1 to head 0
- `+ -` change the byte under head 0
- `< > { }` move the two heads

All other byte values do nothing. Mutation replaces each byte with probability 1/4096 per interaction. A world "comes alive" when its high-order entropy (Shannon entropy minus brotli bits per byte, as cubff computes it) first exceeds 3 bits and its most common programs pass cubff's replication test (score ≥ 5).

**Two head rules.** In the paper's BFF (cubff `bff_noheads`), both heads start at position 0 and execution starts at 0. In cubff's `bff`, the first two bytes of the tape set the heads (`h0 = tape[0] mod 128`, `h1 = tape[1] mod 128`) and execution starts at 2.

**Engine.** [`engine/tol.c`](engine/tol.c) re-implements cubff's interpreter and its SplitMix64 streams for initialisation, pairing and mutation. We validated it against cubff compiled from source, and against a naive build of itself, at 150 checkpoints: random soups with and without mutation, both head rules, and a soup seeded with the paper's hand-written replicator. All were byte-identical ([`engine/validate.sh`](engine/validate.sh)). It adds two exact speed-ups, for 4–5× on a CPU:

- a run of inert bytes is consumed in one step;
- a loop whose state `(pos, h0, h1, tape)` recurs is fast-forwarded by whole periods. Recurrence is detected with a write-free anchor plus Brent's algorithm and a full tape comparison.

A JavaScript port ([`web/bff-engine.js`](web/bff-engine.js)) matches the C engine byte for byte ([`web/test/validate.mjs`](web/test/validate.mjs)). Worlds re-run on a different machine come alive at the same epoch (worlds 29 and 38: 945 and 913 on both).

## 2. The atlas

We ran 58 worlds under the paper's rule and 30 under the genome-set-heads rule, each for up to 16,384 epochs.

- **Paper's rule:** 24 of 58 came alive (41%). The paper reports 40% within 16k epochs with the same settings.
- **Genome-set heads:** 28 of 30 came alive.

For each living world we take the most common exact program that passes the replication test (the "winner") and trace every byte it copies into a random partner, in an independent Python interpreter ([`analysis/bff.py`](analysis/bff.py)).

## 3. Life copies itself backwards

- **Direction.** Under the paper's rule, 23 of 24 winners copy themselves backwards. The exception is world 8, a degenerate frame-shifting copier in a soup overrun by zero bytes. In 22 of the 23 the reversal is exact; world 54 reverses with an offset.
- **The child is the mirror image.** For an exact mirror copier the child is the parent reversed, and the child of the reversed genome is the original again. In every one of these worlds the reversed genome is itself among the most common programs, in almost exactly the same number: 132/131, 82/82, 175/174, and so on.
- **Over the whole soup.** The two strands also split the whole soup evenly. At the end of world 52 we count 45,393 forward and 45,405 reversed copies, classifying every one of its 131,072 programs by the genome's instruction positions.
- **Two machines per genome.** A lineage survives only if its mirror image also works, and the soup satisfies this in two ways. 7 genomes are palindromes. 15 carry two different copy machines. In each of the 22, the mirror strand copies itself with a different copy instruction of the same genome than the forward strand does ([`analysis/bff.py`](analysis/bff.py), `copy_positions`); in 19 of them that instruction sits in the other half of the genome.
- **Any partner.** Mirror copying is also robust: 21 of the 22 exact mirror copiers produce the exact reversed copy with every one of the 2,012 test partners, and world 50 does so with 2,007.

## 4. The whole soup, with controls

Picking the most common genome could be misleading, so we also sampled real pairs from every soup and logged every byte that crossed between partners ([`engine/copygeo.c`](engine/copygeo.c); its traced interpreter must reproduce the engine's tape, and it did in all runs). A copy step runs backwards when, between two consecutive copies by the same instruction, the read and write heads step in opposite directions.

Under the paper's rule, one step left from position 0 already lands in the partner, so accidental copy loops in random soups lean backwards (87%). Under the other rule they have no preference (51%). Life then commits fully: 99.99% backwards in the first case, 0.00% in the second.

*A correction.* The first version of this measure also compared a `.` with a following `,`. That pair swaps source and destination without any head moving. The controls exposed the error: random soups came out 98% "backwards" under both rules. We now compare only consecutive copies by the same instruction, with small wrap-aware steps. The corrected controls are shown above.

## 5. Formal verification

**Model.** [`lean/ProofOfLife/Semantics.lean`](lean/ProofOfLife/Semantics.lean) transcribes BFF for both head rules, including the 8,192-step budget. It agrees with our Python and C interpreters on 240 test tapes that cover every halting path, head wrap-around, byte overflow, and matched and unmatched brackets. That agreement is tested, not proved.

**Method.** [`Abstract.lean`](lean/ProofOfLife/Abstract.lean) defines a second interpreter in which the partner's bytes are unknown. It gives up whenever the concrete run could depend on an unknown byte:

- executing one;
- testing one at `[` or `]`;
- scanning across one while matching brackets.

It also supports an "unknown but non-zero" value. A general soundness theorem, proved once, states: if the abstract run finishes, every concrete tape that agrees with it on its known bytes ends with the same known bytes. For each organism the kernel then evaluates the abstract run with `decide +kernel`, in 512-step chunks. Each chunk ends in a checkpoint, and the kernel re-derives every checkpoint.

**Trust base.** Lean 4.21.0's kernel. There is no `native_decide`, `sorry` or `admit`. Every main theorem depends only on `propext` and `Quot.sound`. A clean rebuild of all 68 modules from an empty cache on a Mac mini (M4), one module at a time, took 44 minutes, and all 60 main theorems report only `[propext, Quot.sound]` ([`lean/verify_report.json`](lean/verify_report.json), [`lean/logs/`](lean/logs)).

| achilles | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| killer | 8 | 0 | 3 |

- **exact** theorems state `∀ B : Vector UInt8 64, run (P ++ B) = P ++ P.reverse` under the paper's rule and `runHeads (P ++ B) = P ++ P` under the other rule. There are 22 of the first kind (including the paper's Figure 4 replicator) and 7 of the second.
- **lineage** adds the same statement for the reversed genome.
- **achilles** is world 50's conditional theorem.
- **killer** entries are concrete runs with a specific partner in which the child does not carry the expected code.

Details are in [`lean/README.md`](lean/README.md) and [`lean/atlas_report.json`](lean/atlas_report.json).

**Not proved.** World 55's mirror strand tests partner byte 62 before overwriting it. The abstract run gives up there, including after a zero/non-zero case split. It copies exactly with 500 of 500 random partners. Three genome-set-heads organisms (1004, 1022, 1403) have no theorem and no killer: their runs depend on the partner, but no partner we tried broke them.

**Why backwards is safer.** The abstract runs show the mechanism directly. A backward copier's loop writes a byte and then tests the byte it just wrote, so it only ever reads its own work. Under the genome-set-heads rule, 21 of 28 organisms test a partner byte while copying. Most test the byte just ahead of the write head, which still belongs to the partner.

## 6. The birth of world 52

The winning lineage of world 52 first appears among the 131,072 programs after epoch 5,858. We re-ran the world from its seed, saved the soup before and after that epoch, recomputed the epoch's pairing (Fisher–Yates on cubff's SplitMix64 stream) and mutation stream, and re-ran the one collision that produced the lineage's first program ([`analysis/birth.py`](analysis/birth.py), result in [`data/birth52.json`](data/birth52.json)).

- **Collision 30,117 of 65,536.** Programs in slots 65,224 (A) and 65,971 (B) are paired.
- **Mutation:** none in this collision.
- **Check:** re-running `A ++ B` reproduces both halves of the saved soup byte for byte.
- **Parents:** neither A nor B copies itself exactly, in either direction, with any of 2,000 random partners.
- **Newborn:** the right half becomes C, an exact mirror copier for every partner, as is C reversed.
- **Growth:** 49 epochs later the lineage has 91,764 members.

The Lean theorem `birth_certificate_world52` combines five statements: the collision, the newborn's any-partner copy, its mirror strand's any-partner copy, and one failing partner for each parent.

## 7. Weak spots

**World 50.** It survives 99.8% of random partners.

- Lean proves `∀ B, B[63] ≠ 0 → run (P ++ B) = P ++ P.reverse` and checks one killer with `B[63] = 0`.
- In tests, every partner with `B[63] = 0` kills it (1,500 of 1,500), and every non-zero value of that byte lets it survive.

**Worlds 1007, 1008 and 1012** (genome-set heads). They show the same pattern at the partner's first byte: 1,500 of 1,500 die when it is 0, and all 255 non-zero values survive. Their killers are Lean-checked. The conditional theorem is not proved for them, because the abstract run keeps meeting partner bytes ahead of the write head. The backward copier is vulnerable at the partner's end and the forward copiers at its start: that is where the write head lands first.

## 8. Act two: a primordial soup of prompts

**Protocol.** 128 texts start as random dictionary words. Each epoch the texts are paired at random. In each pair (A, B), a local Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (4-bit, MLX) gets A as the system prompt and B as the user message, and its reply, cut to 160 characters, overwrites B. There is no task and no selection. We ran 4 worlds for 120 epochs, plus 2 control worlds for 80 epochs with the roles swapped (A as the user message, B as the system prompt, the reply still overwriting B). Raw soups are in [`data/psoup/`](data/psoup).

**Measures.** Each rewrite is classified with character 5-gram Jaccard similarity:

- **copy:** the reply resembles A more than B, with similarity ≥ 0.3;
- **keep:** the reverse;
- **new:** neither.

"Confused" texts match a fixed pattern of apology and clarification phrases.

**Findings.**

- **Confusion attractor.** Within 3 to 6 epochs, 42–59% of each soup says some version of "I'm sorry, could you clarify?". This is an attractor, not a replicator: the model produces it from any nonsense.
- **Heredity without reproduction.** Then the soup moves on to topics. Over the last ten epochs, 69% of rewrites keep their slot's lineage and 6% copy the system prompt.
- **Control.** With the roles swapped, 68% of rewrites copy the acting text. In a chat model, only a text in the user position can reproduce.

These results cover one small model, one framing and 4 + 2 worlds.

## 9. Limitations

- BFF has no chemistry. A "mirror strand" is a reversed byte string, not a complementary one. Analogies to double-stranded genomes are about copying geometry only.
- The atlas has 58 + 30 worlds. Emergence rates carry the corresponding sampling error (41% ± 6% at one standard error).
- The Lean theorems are about the Lean transcription of BFF, and its match with the engine is tested, not proved. They certify single organisms, not soup dynamics.
- The winner of a world is its most common exact replicating program. Soup-wide measures (section 4) and full-soup lineage counts back up the per-genome findings, but a living soup is a cloud of variants.
